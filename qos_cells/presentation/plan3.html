The hard part is not accuracy on 2,638 cells (classical solves that). It is learning from a stream whose feature space is bigger than the memory you can afford, while the class you need is ~1 % of it.

3The quantum technique

Oracle sketching: each record is one phase kick, then it is gone

φ_g = (τ/M) · Σ_t w_t · [g_t = g]
w_t = +p_t/π̂ (rare)  or  −p_t/(1−π̂) (common)
⟹ E[φ_g] ∝ μ₁,g − μ₀,g
⟨Z_anc⟩ = Σ_g a_g² sin φ_g  =  the classifier score

Our addition: the ±1/π̂ importance weights make the sketch imbalance-aware. The mean is unbiased for any π; the price is in the variance (next slides).

Zhao, Zlokapa, Preskill & Huang 2026, arXiv:2604.07639 · no QRAM · p = squared amplitude of the cell

3Our twist · the cost of rare classes

Rare classes cost samples, not qubits: M ∝ 1/(π(1−π))

real classπ1/(π(1−π))M for 10 % error

Two-line derivation: the ±1/π weights leave the mean of the kick unchanged, Σ p·w → μ₁−μ₀, but square inside the variance: E[p²]/π + E[p²]/(1−π) = E[p²]/(π(1−π)). Same two expression profiles in the synthetic, only π changes. Dendritic cells (π = 0.014) pay ×72 in samples over a balanced class, and being rare and subtle is the expensive combination.

5Real world · and an honest ledger

Where it matters, and what it would really cost

quantityall genes · N = 2¹⁵gene pairs · N = 2³⁰

ε = 0.1 · one query · π = 0.014 (dendritic) · gates: one n-controlled phase per record ≈ 2n−1 · 1 MHz logical gates (fault-tolerant, optimistic)

Where this data lives

Cell atlases (CZ CELLxGENE, 10⁷–10⁸ cells), mass cytometry, liquid biopsies: tumour cells at 1 in 10⁶–10⁷. Huge feature spaces, one-pass acquisition, rare but critical classes. Same shape: fraud (0.17 %), AML (0.1 %), QEC decoding (0.14 %).

What we claim · what we don't

Claim: log₂N-qubit memory, N/ε samples, a measured 1/(π(1−π)) rare-class cost, and the primitive runs on a QPU today.
Don't claim: a wall-clock speed-up; that our centroid task inherits the paper's lower bound (open); beating classical on 2,638 cells.

BBackup · likely questions

Q&A register

questionanswer
"Classical does this with 14k floats."Yes, and we show it. The claim is about the regime N ≫ memory (gene pairs, 10⁹ features), where the paper proves a memory–sample trade-off.
"Does your centroid task inherit the lower bound?"Not proven. The paper's bound is for its task families (NOPE, forrelation-type). We say it is open.
"Loading data kills quantum advantages."Sketching is the loading. Each record is one gate and is never stored, so there is no QRAM. The cost is samples ∝ N/ε, which we show.
"Hardware noise?"Known-answer circuits of the same family measure the shrink factor α. We report raw and calibrated values; 11 CZ works raw, 53 CZ needs the calibration.
"Why not train on hardware?"Against the rules, and unnecessary: the sketch accumulation is classical simulation; hardware runs inference with fixed phases.
"Is this just a linear model?"Yes, on purpose. One query = one linear functional. Nonlinear models need more queries Q, at cost Q², the paper's optimal scaling.
"The labels come from clustering the same data."True (Louvain). Fine for a feasibility prototype; stated on the limits slide; a held-out annotated atlas is the next step.
