Plan 1Steps 1–3 · data, challenge, model

Data too big to store: stream it into qubits

① Dataset

KDD Cup 99 · ~500k connections · 41 features · 22 attack types

② Challenge

Huge, fast stream + extreme imbalance (U2R: only 52 records)

③ Why quantum

The data can't be stored; the sketch never stores it. Memory: d → log₂ d.

Plan 1Steps 4–5 · prototype, real world

Three tiers, plus an honest stress test

Tier 1 · classical

Streaming baseline

Memory needed for a given accuracy. Full dataset.

Tier 2 · simulator

Oracle sketching

6–7 qubits, ideal + noisy. Where does it break?

Tier 3 · IQM

Hardware toy

2–3 qubits, 4–16 samples. Inference only.

What we measure
Memory: classical vs quantum
Per-class recall + AUPRC (not accuracy)
Rare attacks: when does the sketch miss them?
Risks · your view?
41 features may be too few: in our quick check the quantum overhead only pays off above ~50–75 features
Circuit depth grows with stream length → only short streams on today's hardware

Real world: intrusion detection on backbone traffic, card-fraud streams, sensor data too large to write to disk.

Plan 2Steps 1–3 · data, challenge, model

When data are quantum states, every shot costs

① Dataset

PennyLane qspin: Ising chains with 4, 8, 16 qubits (a starter dataset)

② Challenge

Few labelled states, and each measurement is expensive

③ Why quantum

Weight sharing → few parameters → fewer states needed (Caro et al. bound √(T/N))

Plan 2Steps 4–5 · prototype, real world

Five small experiments, one fair rival

E1

Learning curve

Accuracy vs number of labelled states

E2

Bound check

Does the gap follow √(T/N)?

E3

Shot budget

Accuracy vs shots per state

E4

Size transfer

Train at 8 qubits, test at 16

E5

Kernels

Quantum vs classical kernel geometry

Plan details
Laptop simulation, ≤16 qubits
Optional: frozen 4-qubit QCNN on IQM
Fallback: unsupervised anomaly detection (no labels)
Risks · your view?
Classical shadows already solve these phases well, so we claim constants, not exponential gains
Shot crossover and 8→16 transfer are untested: a null result is possible

Real world: characterising quantum devices and materials, where every measurement takes lab time.
