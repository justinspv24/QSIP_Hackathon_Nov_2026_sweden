3Argue for a quantum technique

Quantum oracle sketching: each sample nudges the qubits, then it is gone

Based on Zhao, Zlokapa, Preskill & Huang (2026), arXiv:2604.07639. No QRAM: the data is never loaded into quantum memory, only streamed through.

Rare attacks: the honest failure test

4Prototype results · real hardware

IQM run: does the mechanism survive noise?

Our own caveat

Each streamed sample adds gates, so circuit depth grows with T. On today's hardware only short streams stay faithful.

What we show: the mechanism works at small scale on a real QPU, and how the error grows with T.

No training on hardware · inference with frozen parameters

5Connect to the real world

Where streams are too big to keep

Intrusion detection

Backbone routers see millions of connections per second. Storing them all is not an option.

Payment fraud

Card transactions arrive continuously; fraud is ~0.17% of them. Same two properties.

Sensors & science

Telescopes, detectors and IoT fleets produce more data than they can write to disk.

~20

qubits for 10⁶ features

Realistic sizing: the qubit count is small. The bottleneck is circuit depth over long streams, so this needs error-corrected hardware to run at full scale.

Takeaway

Data too big to store
→ stream it into ⌈log₂ d⌉ qubits.

Works

Memory scales with log of features, not features.

Breaks

Very rare classes, and long streams on noisy hardware.
